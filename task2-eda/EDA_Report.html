Hypothesis test: ratings and review counts

Question: Are higher review counts associated with higher public ratings?

H₀: rating and review count have no monotonic association. H₁: they have a monotonic association.

On 99 complete records, Spearman’s rank correlation was ρ = -0.305. A two-sided permutation test with 20,000 shuffles gave p = 0.0024. This is evidence of a modest negative association in this sheet. It does not show that review count causes rating changes, and the rating values are rounded with many ties.

Method: Spearman rank correlation, two-sided permutation test (20,000 shuffles, fixed seed). Average ranks handle tied ratings; complete records only. This is an association within this workbook, not a causal effect or a measure of lead conversion.

Data issues to address

  1. Remove the fully blank row and the 31 unused columns. Reconcile the file’s 106-lead label with its 108 named records.
  2. Give the duplicate Phone headers distinct names and confirm whether they mean primary and secondary contact numbers. Keep phone values as text.
  3. Standardize Category and Status values. Use a controlled set of update outcomes instead of free-text notes, and reconcile notes with status.
  4. Confirm what Last Contacted means. Its 5 populated values all share one date, which may represent an export/default date rather than separate contact events.
  5. Add explicit outcome fields such as contact attempt, response, qualified, and converted if the goal is to evaluate the lead funnel.
  6. Keep missing ratings and review counts as missing until a source can validate them. Do not replace them with zero.

What this dataset cannot answer: every populated Status is “New,” and there are no response, qualification, or conversion outcomes. It cannot support conversion-rate analysis or identify which categories produce successful leads.
